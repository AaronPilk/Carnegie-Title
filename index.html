ii. Commercial

Commercial Title Insurance

Unlike residential properties, commercial ones usually carry a hefty price tag and involve intricate negotiation procedures. Consequently, safeguarding your investment to its fullest extent, commensurate with its value, is paramount.

What property type requires title insurance?

Although title insurance is common across all real estate transactions, its significance escalates in commercial real estate dealings due to the typically elevated purchase prices and intricate purchase agreements involved. Given the complexity of commercial real estate agreements, it is imperative to ascertain that your commercial title insurance policy provides comprehensive coverage to safeguard against any potential occurrences.

Why do I need commercial title insurance?

Commercial title insurance safeguards the investor in case any complications arise concerning the property's title. Every investment carries a degree of risk, and in real estate, encountering issues with a property's title is always a possibility. These issues can stem from sources such as negligence, clerical errors, or even fraud. In the absence of a title insurance policy, the property investor risks losing their investment without any financial recourse.

Types of policies and who pays.

There are two types of title insurance: the lender's policy and the owner's policy. The lender's policy shields the lender, while the owner's policy shields the property owner. If the buyer is utilizing a lender, they are responsible for purchasing both policies. The lender's policy remains valid for the duration of the loan, whereas the owner's policy provides coverage for as long as the individual owns the property. It's essential to understand that the lender's policy solely safeguards the lender. Therefore, we strongly advise the buyer to obtain an owner's policy to protect their financial interests.

Lender's vs. owner's policy

A lender's policy is required for nearly every financed transaction; it protects the lender's interest, and only up to the loan balance. An owner's policy is optional — but, candidly, the more important of the two. It protects you and your equity for as long as you or your heirs hold an interest in the property.

The premium is paid once, at closing. The protection is permanent.
